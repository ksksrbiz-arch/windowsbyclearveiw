We cross the river. Portland sits where the Willamette meets the Columbia, with the West Hills rising on one side of the river and flatter ground on the other — Vancouver is a short run north across the Columbia from either side. At well over 600,000 people it is Oregon's largest city.

## Housing in Portland

The housing stock is the part we know best — old Portland four-squares, bungalows, and mid-century ranches with single-pane sash or tired aluminum. It's an older average building stock than most of the Clark County towns on this list, simply because Portland was built out earlier and more densely.

## What tends to go wrong

On a 1910s house the frame is often out of square and the sill has taken decades of water, which pushes a job toward full-frame rather than an insert (see [full-frame vs. insert](/guides/full-frame-vs-insert) for the actual line between the two). On mid-century ranches, it's more often a single-pane or early-double-pane unit that's simply lost its efficiency, not a structural problem with the opening.

## What we do here

Portland's rain is the same rain. What's different is the age of the openings, and we tell you which method an opening needs when we measure, not after the units are ordered. Historic districts and design review are their own conversation in Portland — if your house sits in one, say so on the first call, since it changes what we can put in the opening and how long the approval takes.
