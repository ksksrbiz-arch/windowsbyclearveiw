Camas incorporated in 1906 as a paper-mill town, and that history still shapes the city: an older downtown core with genuine single-pane sash mixed in among whatever got swapped in over the decades, and newer growth pushing north and east toward Lacamas Lake. The city has grown past 27,000 residents, and most of that growth sits in the newer subdivisions rather than the old mill core.

## Housing in Camas

Two very different housing pictures in one city. Downtown and the streets around it carry original early-1900s construction — settled frames, older sash, sills that have taken a century of Camas rain. North and east Camas is the opposite: recent subdivision construction, generally solid windows, with the occasional builder-grade unit worth a second look.

## What tends to go wrong

In the old mill-town core, it's frame condition more than glass — a sill or jamb that has moved enough over a hundred years that a straightforward insert won't sit right, which is when full-frame is the honest answer (see [full-frame vs. insert](/guides/full-frame-vs-insert)). In the newer subdivisions, problems are rarer and usually smaller: a seal that failed early, or hardware that never worked quite right out of the box.
