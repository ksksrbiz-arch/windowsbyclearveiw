There are two honest ways to put a new window in an old opening. The right one depends on the frame that is already there, not on a sales package.

## Insert, or pocket, replacement

The old sash comes out. The existing frame stays. A new window is built to sit inside that frame.

Choose this when:

- The frame is solid, square, and dry
- Exterior trim and siding are worth keeping
- You want less disruption inside the rooms

Watch-outs: you give up a little glass area, and any rot hiding in that old frame stays hidden. If we see soft wood or a racked opening, we will not force an insert.

## Full-frame replacement

The entire old window comes out to the rough opening. We flash the opening, set a new unit, and close the interior and exterior finishes back up.

Choose this when:

- The frame is rotten, out of square, or aluminum that has had it
- You want to change the look of the exterior casing
- Water has already been in the wall

It is more work. It is also the only way to start over at the opening — which is what a wet Washington wall often needs.

## Signs a frame won't take an insert

An insert only works if the existing frame is doing its job structurally. Walk the opening and check for:

- **Soft wood at the sill or jamb** — press with a screwdriver handle, not just a look. If it gives, water has been inside the wall.
- **A sash that racks instead of sliding square** — the opening itself has shifted, not just the hardware.
- **Visible gap or daylight at a corner** — the frame is no longer square enough to seal a new insert against.
- **Aluminum frames with heavy pitting or corrosion** — common on 1970s-80s Vancouver-area homes, and rarely worth insetting into.

Any one of these usually means full-frame, whatever the rest of the house gets.

## How we decide on site

We do not pick a method from a photo of the glass. We look at:

- The sill and stool with a probe, not just an eye
- Whether the sash still closes square
- How the exterior cladding meets the old casing
- Whether you want the same sightlines or a different window style

Then the estimate lists the method per opening, not one price that hides a worse job.

## What this means for cost and time

Inserts are usually faster and cost less per opening. Full-frame takes more crew time and more finish work. A mixed house is normal. Read [what a Washington project costs](/guides/window-replacement-cost-washington) for ranges, then get a measure so the number is about *your* openings.
