Short answer: **it depends on your city or county.** Washington's state codes set what a replacement window has to meet. Your local building department decides whether you need a permit to put one in. Where they say no permit is needed, the window still has to meet the same code.

In every building department below, **making an opening bigger, smaller, or new needs a permit.**

## What the building departments say

We checked each of these building departments' published pages in October 2026. Rules and wording change, so treat this as a starting point and confirm with the building department for your address.

- **Unincorporated Clark County** (homes outside a city, such as Brush Prairie). A permit is required to replace windows, even the same size in the same place. The county issues a roofing, siding and windows permit for it.
- **Camas.** The city's permit list shows doors and windows as needing a permit. For a like-for-like replacement, no plans are required and the permit is generally issued the day you apply. A new opening or a change in size needs plans.
- **Battle Ground.** The city lists window replacement of the same size as work that does not need a permit.
- **Ridgefield.** The city lists window and door replacement of the same size as work that does not need a permit. A different size does.
- **La Center.** The city lists window replacement among the projects that need a building permit.

Other cities have their own rules, including Vancouver, Washougal and Woodland. They are not listed here. Ask the building department for your address.

## What a replacement window has to meet, permit or not

A permit is only one part of this. These rules apply to the window itself.

- **Energy.** The Washington State Energy Code (2021 edition, in effect statewide since March 15, 2024) says that when some or all of an existing window is replaced, including the sash and glass, the new unit has to meet the code's U-factor limit. For homes in our area that limit is **0.30 or lower**. If you are replacing several windows, the code lets you average them by area. Storm windows added over an existing window and window film put on existing single-pane glass are exceptions. Ask any installer for the U-factor of the window they are quoting.
- **Escape (egress) windows.** Clark County's permit handout says egress windows still have to meet the building code's egress rules when they are replaced. It says the sill-height rule does not apply when you replace a window in the same opening.
- **Safety glass.** The same handout notes that some windows may need safety glazing, depending on where they sit in the house.

## When the opening changes

A larger opening, a smaller one, or a window where there was none moves framing. That is structural work, and it is the case every building department above asks for a permit for. Our guide on [what each opening needs](/guides/what-your-openings-need) covers what we look at when we measure.

## Questions worth asking your building department

1. Do I need a permit to replace windows in the same size openings at my address?
2. If I do, are plans required, or is there a shorter process for like-for-like windows?
3. Will an inspection be needed, and who schedules it?
4. If any opening gets bigger or smaller, what do you need to see?

If you live in a neighborhood with a homeowners association, it can have its own approval step that is separate from the city's.
